# BRIEF-001 · Rewive project brief

The founding statement of what Rewive is, what it refuses to be, and the
mechanism that makes it work. Every other document descends from this one. When
a decision elsewhere contradicts this brief, this brief wins — or it gets
amended deliberately, with a note saying why.

---

## Entry 01 · The cycle this exists to fix

Every operating company runs on the same monthly rhythm:

1. The period closes.
2. Finance and the business spend **three to four days building the pack** —
   reconciling, charting, writing the narrative of what happened.
3. The **Business Review Meeting** happens, usually between the 7th and the
   10th. The room is told what happened.
4. A list of **action items** comes out of it.
5. Then the month happens to everybody, and the list waits for the next BRM.

Steps 1–3 are expensive, well-staffed, and genuinely good. Step 4 is where the
value was supposed to be created. Step 5 is where it is lost.

By the next BRM, some items were done, some were overtaken, some were quietly
renegotiated in a corridor, and some are simply raised again — with the same
words, by the same person, to the same silence. The pack gets better every
month. The follow-through does not.

**The gap is not visibility. It is accountability.** A red number on a
dashboard is nobody's obligation. It waits for a meeting, becomes a deck, and
drifts for a quarter.

## Entry 02 · Why action items drift

Drift is not laziness, and it is rarely disagreement. It is what happens when
**attention is the only thing holding an action item in place** — and attention
is the scarcest resource in the company. Five patterns account for most of it:

| # | Pattern | What actually happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Market drift** | Something starts burning. Focus moves to the fire, and every item that isn't on fire silently loses its owner. |
| 2 | **Sales is up** | Growth becomes the story. Nobody is willing to be the person interrogating margin while the top line is celebrated. |
| 3 | **Margin push** | The correction over-swings: protecting margin on one line cannibalises another, and no one is accountable for the net. |
| 4 | **Inventory optimisation** | The question "what did we produce against what actually sold?" spans planning, production and commercial — so it belongs to everyone and therefore to no one. |
| 5 | **Logistics and distribution** | Deeply operational teams are measured on today's movement. A structural fix loses to today's trucks, every single day. |

The common shape: **people own some and disown a lot.** Not dishonestly —
an item without a clock, an owner of record, and a definition of done is not
really an item. It is a sentence in a document.

### The wider catalogue

Those five are the ones customers name first. The same shape appears far more
widely, and grouping by **mechanism** rather than by symptom matters: a
mechanism predicts drift in cases nobody has named yet.

**A · Attention — the fire wins**

| Pattern | What actually happens |
|---|---|
| **Market drift** | Something starts burning. Every item that isn't on fire silently loses its owner. |
| **Launch fade** | A new product owns the room for a quarter, then the next launch arrives and the first one's numbers stop being anyone's morning question. |
| **Peak-season absorption** | Everything defers to the season. The deferred list is never formally re-opened — it simply stops being mentioned. |

**B · Ownership — the metric and the lever sit with different people**

| Pattern | What actually happens |
|---|---|
| **Working capital** | Finance measures DSO, DPO and inventory days. Commercial and operations move them. The owner of the number cannot act; the people who can act are not measured on it. |
| **Inventory optimisation** | "What did we produce against what actually sold?" spans planning, production and commercial — so it belongs to everyone and therefore to no one. |
| **Supplier performance** | The fix requires a conversation with an external party. Absorbing the cost internally is structurally easier than escalating it. |
| **Reorg vacuum** | A reorganisation silently orphans mandates. Nobody notices until the number does. |

**C · Time — the feedback arrives after accountability has dissolved**

| Pattern | What actually happens |
|---|---|
| **Trade spend and promo leakage** | Accruals settle months later. By the time the true cost lands, the promo's owner has moved on to three other things. |
| **Capex benefit realisation** | The business case promised a number. The approval was the event; the outcome never gets checked. |
| **Compliance follow-ups** | Distant deadlines feel safe, right up until the scramble. |

**D · Narrative — the story of the quarter makes a question unaskable**

| Pattern | What actually happens |
|---|---|
| **Sales is up** | Growth becomes the story. Nobody wants to be the person interrogating margin while the top line is celebrated. |
| **Margin push** | The correction over-swings: protecting margin on one line cannibalises another, and no one is accountable for the net. |
| **Operational primacy** | Deeply operational teams are measured on today's movement. A structural fix loses to today's trucks, every single day. |

**E · Normalisation — a bad number quietly becomes the baseline**

| Pattern | What actually happens |
|---|---|
| **Scrap and waste run-rate** | "That's just our scrap rate." A number nobody defends and nobody challenges. |
| **Forecast bias** | Each individual forecast is defensible. The chronic bias across all of them is nobody's item. |
| **Agency-labour creep** | Temporary cover becomes permanent. No single increment is large enough to trigger a review. |

**F · Memory — the organisation cannot recall what it already decided**

| Pattern | What actually happens |
|---|---|
| **Re-raised as new** | Decided in a prior BRM, never actioned, raised again in the same words by the same person to the same silence. |
| **Unassessed decisions** | The fix shipped. Whether it moved the number was never established, so the next decision is made with no evidence. |

### What answers each mechanism

The taxonomy earns its place because each family maps to a specific part of the
loop. If a proposed feature does not answer one of these, it is probably not
solving the problem this product exists for.

| Mechanism | What catches it |
|---|---|
| **A · Attention** | The clock belongs to the finding, not the meeting. A counterpart cannot be distracted, promoted, or reassigned. |
| **B · Ownership** | Every mandate has exactly one accountable human owner; matrix escalation lets one drift be held by two chains; no operation may leave a mandate ownerless. |
| **C · Time** | Recovery targets are watched to closure, and an assessor returns later for the verdict — long after human attention has moved on. |
| **D · Narrative** | Mandates are held independently. A mandate does not stop being watched because a different number is good news. |
| **E · Normalisation** | A mandate is an explicit standing target. Drift from it raises a finding regardless of how long the drift has been true, and Park requires a re-alert rule rather than silence. |
| **F · Memory** | The Decision Ledger, and the re-raise rate as a first-class measure. |

Rewive's premise is that the fix is not more discipline, better templates, or a
stricter chair. It is **structural**: give every number an owner who cannot
quietly stop holding it, and a counterpart that never looks away.

## Entry 03 · What Rewive is

> **The system of record for operational decisions.**
> **Nothing drifts unanswered.**

Dashboards show you the number. Rewive **holds every number twice** — a person
and an AI counterpart on the same mandate — so the moment it drifts, a decision
is demanded, watched to closure, and remembered.

The person decides. The counterpart never looks away. Silence escalates up your
real org chart on an SLA.

That is the whole product in three sentences. Everything below is mechanism.

## Entry 04 · The loop

**Sense → Find → Decide → Act → Close.** It runs whether or not anyone is
watching.

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Sense** | Each counterpart watches the live signals behind its mandates continuously — not on a reporting cadence. |
| **Find** | When reality drifts from a mandate, the counterpart raises a **finding**, prices the impact, and traces it up the P&L to the intent it threatens. The SLA clock starts. |
| **Decide** | The owner must choose. Nothing proceeds without a decision, and an unanswered finding escalates up the org on its SLA. |
| **Act** | The decision sets the machine in motion: a watched recovery target, a solution broken into tasks for agents and people, or a deliberate, recorded pause. |
| **Close** | **Nothing is "done" until the number is back.** A met recovery target retires the finding; every decision lands in the ledger with a later verdict. Then back to sensing. |

Four decisions are available, and each is a different instruction to the
organization — not a status you set and forget:

- **Accept** — set a measurable recovery target. The counterpart keeps watching
  until the number is truly back, then closes the loop itself.
- **Act** — open a solution, broken into tasks. New work goes to agents,
  existing agents are reused, humans own the rest.
- **Park** — a known issue, held on a re-alert rule. It comes back, louder, the
  moment it crosses the line you set.
- **Dismiss** — not real. A reason is required, and the reason is not
  paperwork: it tunes the counterpart so it learns what not to raise.

## Entry 05 · The five primitives

Everything in the product is one of five things. They are
industry-parameterized, so the vocabulary is the customer's from the first
screen.

| Primitive | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Mandate** | A thing the company is trying to make true. "Case fill above 97%." "Denial rate under 6%." Every mandate has exactly one accountable human owner. |
| **Signal** | A live data feed that verifies a mandate. A mandate with no signal behind it is shown **blind** — never faked. |
| **Counterpart (agent)** | The AI holder of the same mandate. Watches the signals continuously, raises findings, prices and traces impact, holds the watch on recovery targets. |
| **Finding** | A demand for a decision, with an impact path and a clock. Not a notification. |
| **Decision Ledger** | The company's memory of judgment — every decision, its rationale, and a later assessor verdict: worked, didn't, or too early to tell. |

## Entry 06 · What "held twice" means operationally

Two owners per number: a person and an agent. That is the mechanism that makes
disowning structurally impossible.

- **The counterpart cannot get bored, promoted, or reassigned.** It watches the
  same mandate on the 3rd of the month and the 27th, during a fire and during a
  quiet week.
- **The clock belongs to the finding, not to the meeting.** Drift is caught in
  the moment, not on the calendar.
- **Silence is not an outcome.** An unanswered finding escalates up the real org
  chart — divisions, functions, matrix lines and all. One drift can be held by
  two chains and be visible to both.
- **The requirement is a decision, not attention.** An owner's *disposition* is
  what the system demands. "I looked at it" is not a state the product has.
- **Executive queues are empty by design.** An executive sees a finding only
  when their organization went silent. That is the point.

## Entry 07 · The organization that runs on agents

The ambition is a full operating organization running on agents. The framing
that keeps it honest:

**Agents do the continuous work. Humans make the calls.**

Agents are good at what humans cannot sustain: watching every signal without
pause, pricing an impact the same way every time, tracing a causal path through
the P&L, remembering to follow up on week three, and verifying that a number
actually came back. Humans are irreplaceable at the thing the whole loop exists
to produce: **the judgment call**, made by someone accountable for it.

> This is not automation replacing judgment — it is **judgment made
> unavoidable**. The counterpart brings the finding; the human owns the call;
> the ledger remembers whether it worked.

Following up is no longer a human job. Deciding always is.

## Entry 08 · What this does to the BRM

Rewive does not replace the Business Review Meeting. It changes what the
meeting is *for*.

| | Today | With Rewive |
|---|---|---|
| Prep | 3–4 days assembling what happened | The operating picture is already live and reconciled |
| The meeting | Being told what happened | Reviewing what was **decided** and what it produced |
| Output | A list of action items | Decisions already on the ledger, with clocks already running |
| Between meetings | The list waits | The loop runs — findings raised, decided, watched, closed |
| Next meeting | "Where are we on that?" | The verdict is already recorded: worked, didn't, too early |

Most FP&A questions asked in that room become **standing mandates with live
answers** rather than requests that generate another three days of work. The
question "what did we produce against what sold?" stops being an analysis
someone owes the room, and becomes a mandate a counterpart watches
continuously.

## Entry 09 · What the product produces

The Decision Ledger. **Auditable by a CFO, not admired in a review meeting.**

Most companies can tell you what they measured. Almost none can tell you what
they decided, or whether it worked. The ledger records every decision the
moment it is made, priced going in, verified coming out, with an assessor
returning later for the verdict.

That artifact is the reason to buy. Everything else is how it gets made.

## Entry 10 · Scope — what Rewive is not

Guarding these boundaries is most of the work of staying on-idea.

- **Not a BI or dashboard product.** It does not compete on charts. If the
  answer to a request is "add a visualisation", the request is probably wrong.
- **Not a task manager.** Tasks exist only downstream of a decision. A task
  without a finding behind it does not belong in the system.
- **Not RPA or workflow automation.** The product does not aim to execute the
  business; it aims to make sure the business answers.
- **Not a chat assistant over company data.** Findings are raised by mandate
  and signal, not by someone thinking to ask.
- **Not an alerting tool.** An alert can be ignored without consequence. A
  finding cannot — that difference is the entire product.
- **Not a replacement for human accountability.** Ownership is never
  transferred to an agent. It can be transferred to another person, and that
  transfer is itself a recorded event.

## Entry 11 · How success is measured

The metrics the product must be able to show its own customer:

| Measure | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Median time from finding raised to decision** | The core promise: drift meets a decision fast. |
| **Decision win rate** | Of assessed decisions, how many moved the number back. The ledger's credibility. |
| **Findings closed by recovery target met** | Proof that "done" means the number came back, not that someone clicked. |
| **Escalations that reached the top** | Should trend toward zero. A busy CEO queue means the organization went silent. |
| **Mandate coverage with live signals** | Blind mandates are honest but useless. Coverage is the health of the picture. |
| **Re-raise rate** | The same drift returning is the clearest measure of decisions that did not stick. |

## Entry 12 · Non-negotiables

The list to check any proposed feature against. If a change breaks one of
these, it is off-idea regardless of how good it looks.

1. **Every mandate has exactly one accountable human owner.** Never zero, never
   an agent, never a committee.
2. **Every finding demands a decision.** There is no "acknowledge and move on"
   state that stops the clock without a recorded choice.
3. **Silence escalates.** Always up the real org chart, on an SLA.
4. **Nothing is "done" until the number is back.** Closure is a measured
   recovery target, not a status.
5. **Every decision lands in the ledger, and gets a verdict later.**
6. **A mandate with no live signal is shown blind.** Never inferred, never
   faked. Trust is the product.
7. **Accountability transfers, never bypasses.** An admin cannot answer for a
   mandate they do not own — they take ownership first, and that is a recorded
   event.
8. **Dismissal requires a reason, and the reason teaches the agent.**
9. **Each seat sees only what it holds.** No generic "admin sees everything"
   view.
10. **The loop runs whether or not anyone is watching.**

## Entry 13 · Language rules

The product's credibility depends on consistent, non-inflated language.

**Keep verbatim:**
- "Every mandate, held twice." *(landing page and guide finale only — in-app
  copy says "every number has two owners — a person and an agent")*
- "The company's memory of judgment."
- "Nothing is 'done' until the number is back."
- "Nothing drifts unanswered." *(statement form — never "makes someone answer
  for it", which reads as blame)*

**In-product vocabulary** — the plain-language pass; internal identifiers
deliberately lag these words:

| Concept | UI says |
|---|---|
| disposition | **Decide** / "needs a decision" / "decided" |
| acknowledge | **Park** — "known issue; re-alerts if it gets worse" |
| abandon | **Dismiss** — "not real; your reason tunes the agent" |
| exit condition | **recovery target** |
| trip-wire | **re-alert rule** |
| sense (data feed) | **signal** |

**Avoid:**
- **"Shadow organization."** It is a useful internal mental model for how the
  counterpart layer mirrors the real org, and it may be used in internal
  conversation — but it must never reach product copy, the site, or customer
  material. It reads as something operating behind the company's back, which
  inverts the trust the product is selling.
- **"Agentic operating model"** — survives only as story-page essay framing.
- **Any humans-versus-agents ranking.** Not "humans as a rider aid", not
  "agents do the real work". The relationship is a division of labour between
  continuous attention and accountable judgment, and the copy should always
  read that way.
- Blame framing anywhere. The product makes decisions unavoidable; it does not
  catch people out.
